Overview

Why this work matters

Many existing graph generators focus on tree-like structures, yet real vascular networks may contain cycles. This work introduces a two-stage denoising diffusion approach that first generates 3D node coordinates and then samples edge connectivity to build diverse, anatomically plausible vessel graphs.

Approach

How the method works

01

Denoise node positions

A coordinate diffusion model samples spatial locations for graph vertices in 3D.

02

Generate connectivity

A subsequent discrete diffusion model predicts graph edges while keeping the generated node coordinates fixed.

03

Evaluate generated graphs

Compare generated vessel networks with real-world vascular graphs, including their geometry and connectivity.

Research directions

Applications and evaluation

Microscopic capillaries

Model vascular networks in which cycles are a natural topological feature.

Cerebral vasculature

Generate major-vessel structures including the Circle of Willis.

Synthetic graph modeling

Study diverse, previously unseen vessel graph samples and their structural statistics.
