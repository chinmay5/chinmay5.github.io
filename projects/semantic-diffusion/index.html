Overview

Why this work matters

Biological networks such as cerebral vessels and lung airways are sparse, spatially embedded graphs with strong structural constraints. This work introduces a semantically consistent graph diffusion method that uses edge-deletion noising and a stochastic projection operator to enforce plausibility during sampling.

Approach

How the method works

01

Model 3D nodes

Represent the spatial layout of vessels and airways as node coordinates and sparse graph connectivity.

02

Diffuse sparse edges

Use an edge-deletion-based noising process suited to biological networks, followed by discrete edge generation.

03

Project to valid graphs

Apply a stochastic projection operator during sampling to correct semantic or structural inconsistencies.

Research directions

Applications and evaluation

Circle of Willis

Generate graphs of major cerebral vessels with anatomically meaningful connectivity.

Lung airways

Model realistic branching airway trees under relevant structural constraints.

Downstream utility

Evaluate graph labeling and link prediction in addition to graph quality.
